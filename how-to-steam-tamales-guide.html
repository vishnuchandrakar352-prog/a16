How to Steam Tamales

Steaming is where tamales become tamales. Get the pot, the water and the timing right, and the masa turns tender and pulls cleanly from the husk.

Uno

Choose a pot

A tall stockpot with a steamer insert, called a tamalera, is ideal. Any large pot works if you can raise a rack above the water. Line the rack with spare husks to protect the bottom row from direct steam.

Dos

Stand them up

Arrange the tamales upright, open end up, leaning against each other. Pack them snugly but not tightly, since the masa expands as it cooks. If the pot is not full, fill the gaps with balls of foil so they stay standing.

Tres

Water and heat

Add water to just below the rack.

  • Cover the tamales with more husks and a damp towel, then the lid.
  • Bring to a boil, then lower to a steady simmer.
  • A coin in the water rattles while there is still water; if it goes quiet, top up with boiling water.
Cuatro

How long to steam

Medium tamales usually need about an hour to an hour and a half; large ones can take two hours. Frozen uncooked tamales need extra time. Avoid lifting the lid too often, because each look lets the steam escape.

Cinco

The doneness test

Take one tamal out and let it rest for five minutes. Unwrap it: if the husk peels away cleanly and the masa is firm, not sticky, the batch is ready. If it clings, return it and steam for another fifteen minutes. Let the pot rest off the heat before serving.

consejo

Tamales firm up as they rest. Turn off the heat and leave them in the covered pot for fifteen minutes before serving.
